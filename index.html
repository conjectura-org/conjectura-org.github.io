Conjectura

Push the boundary of knowledge

A community corpus of open questions in mathematics and theoretical computer science, each stated precisely enough that a machine can check an answer.

What that means, concretely

Every question here has exactly one canonical Lean 4 statement — its API. A named researcher signs off that the Lean means what the mathematics means; after that, whether a proof is correct stops being a matter of opinion. 

/-- Every non-trivial zero of the Riemann zeta function has real part 1/2. -/
def goal : Prop :=
  ∀ s : ℂ, riemannZeta s = 0 → 0 < s.re → s.re < 1 → s.re = 1 / 2
  1. Someone asks a question in English. No Lean required.
  2. A maintainer writes the canonical statement and puts their name on it.
  3. Anyone downloads that exact file and tries to prove it.
  4. The Lean kernel decides, and the axioms used are audited.
  5. The answer is written back into English, so it can be read.

Where the project is

Not open yet. The corpus, the verifier and the site are built and running; what is missing is the part no software can supply — researchers willing to vouch that a formal statement says what their field means. That is the gate, and it is deliberate: a machine-checked answer to the wrong question is worth nothing.

Maintaining a field

A maintainer owns a subject area and answers one question about each statement in it: does this Lean say what the mathematics means? You never read a proof — that is the kernel's job. Your name appears on every statement you approve, permanently and with a link. It is a citable contribution.
